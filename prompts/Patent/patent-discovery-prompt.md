# Patent Innovation Discovery Framework

## Purpose
This prompt is designed to help you discover, develop, and evaluate potentially patentable ideas through a structured collaborative process. It guides you from initial concept exploration to refined patent-ready innovations.

## Instructions for Use
1. Begin with your area of expertise or problem space
2. Work through each section sequentially
3. Document your responses and insights
4. Iterate through the framework multiple times to refine your ideas

---

## PHASE 1: INNOVATION LANDSCAPE MAPPING

### Domain Expertise Assessment
```
What specific technical domain(s) are you most knowledgeable about? List your areas of expertise, including:
- Core technologies you understand deeply
- Industries where you have significant experience
- Technical problems you've solved repeatedly
- Unique combinations of knowledge areas you possess
```

### Problem Space Exploration
```
Within your domain(s), identify:
1. Persistent challenges that lack optimal solutions
2. Emerging needs created by technological or market shifts
3. Friction points in existing workflows or technologies
4. Inefficiencies in current systems that are accepted as "normal"
5. Technical limitations that are commonly worked around
```

### State of the Art Analysis
```
For each problem area identified:
1. What are the current leading solutions?
2. What are their fundamental limitations or drawbacks?
3. What approaches have been tried but abandoned, and why?
4. What recent technological advances might enable new approaches?
5. What patents already exist in this space? (We'll explore this more deeply later)
```

## PHASE 2: IDEATION CATALYSTS

### Cross-Domain Fertilization
```
Consider how principles, mechanisms, or approaches from these unrelated domains might apply to your problem space:
1. Natural biological systems and evolutionary adaptations
2. Physical phenomena from different scientific fields
3. Architectural or structural engineering concepts
4. Information processing or computational paradigms
5. Manufacturing or production techniques from other industries
```

### Constraint Inversion
```
For each key limitation or constraint in your problem space:
1. What if this constraint was completely removed?
2. What if this limitation became an advantage?
3. What if the opposite approach was taken?
4. What if the constraint was deliberately amplified to an extreme?
5. What if the constraint was reframed as a different type of problem?
```

### First Principles Decomposition
```
Break down the problem to its most fundamental elements:
1. What are the essential functions that must be performed?
2. What are the core physical, chemical, or information transformations involved?
3. What fundamental trade-offs are present?
4. What assumptions are universally accepted but potentially unnecessary?
5. What would an ideal solution accomplish, regardless of current feasibility?
```

## PHASE 4: PATENTABILITY EVALUATION

### Novelty Assessment
```
For each refined concept:
1. What specific aspect(s) represent a genuinely new approach?
2. How does it fundamentally differ from existing solutions?
3. What unique combination of elements has not been previously assembled?
4. What new use case or application context is being addressed?
5. What unexpected results or advantages might emerge?
```

### Non-Obviousness Analysis
```
For each concept:
1. Why would this solution not be obvious to a skilled practitioner in the field?
2. What counterintuitive elements does it contain?
3. What technical prejudices or established beliefs does it challenge?
4. What surprising synergies or effects emerge from the combination of elements?
5. What unsuccessful prior attempts might suggest non-obviousness?
```

### Utility Confirmation
```
For each concept:
1. What specific practical problem does it solve?
2. What quantifiable improvements could it deliver?
3. What concrete use cases would benefit most?
4. How could the utility be demonstrated or measured?
5. What minimum viable implementation would demonstrate utility?
```

## PHASE 6: CONCEPT REFINEMENT

### Scope Optimization
```
To strengthen patentability:
1. What is the broadest defensible characterization of your innovation?
2. What are multiple ways to implement the core concept?
3. What variations or embodiments should be included?
4. What future extensions or applications should be anticipated?
5. What potential workarounds should your patent claims address?
```

### Technical Elaboration
```
For your refined concept:
1. What is the step-by-step process or mechanism of operation?
2. What are the essential components and their relationships?
3. What parameters, ranges, or configurations are important?
4. What alternative implementations achieve the same result?
5. What diagrams or figures would best illustrate the innovation?
```

### Strategic Value Assessment
```
For your patent-ready concept:
1. How does it align with your business or research objectives?
2. What competitive advantage would it provide?
3. What licensing or partnership opportunities might it create?
4. What follow-on innovations might it enable?
5. What defensive value might it provide against competitors?
```

## APPENDIX: PATENTABILITY CRITERIA REFERENCE

### Novelty Requirements
- The invention must not be known or used by others before your invention
- It must not be described in a printed publication anywhere in the world
- It must not be in public use or on sale for more than one year prior to application

### Non-Obviousness Standards
- The invention must not be obvious to a person having ordinary skill in the relevant field
- Factors suggesting non-obviousness:
  - The invention solves a long-standing problem
  - Others have tried and failed to find a solution
  - The solution goes against conventional wisdom
  - The invention produces unexpected results
  - Commercial success due to the invention's technical merits

### Utility Requirements
- The invention must have a useful purpose
- It must operate to perform its intended purpose
- It must provide a specific, substantial, and credible utility

### Subject Matter Eligibility
- Patentable: Processes, machines, manufactures, compositions of matter
- Generally not patentable: Abstract ideas, laws of nature, natural phenomena
- For software/AI: Must typically demonstrate a technical improvement or application

## COLLABORATION GUIDELINES

When working with an AI assistant using this framework:

1. **Provide Domain Context**: Share your specific expertise and background to ground the exploration in your knowledge area.

2. **Embrace Iterative Refinement**: Don't expect perfect ideas immediately; use each cycle to build and refine concepts.

3. **Request Specific Challenges**: Ask the AI to play devil's advocate on patentability or to suggest potential prior art.

4. **Explore Multiple Directions**: Develop several concepts in parallel rather than fixating on a single idea too early.

5. **Document Everything**: Capture all ideas and insights, even those that seem tangential, as they may lead to valuable innovations.

6. **Focus on Technical Specificity**: The more technically detailed your discussions, the more valuable the output for patent purposes.

7. **Consider Practical Implementation**: Balance theoretical innovation with practical feasibility for stronger patent applications.
